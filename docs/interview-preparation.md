# Interview Preparation

Interview preparation is most effective when it connects professional experience to clear engineering judgement. For a senior .NET and Azure engineering role, the goal is to show how technical decisions improve delivery confidence, production reliability, team capability, and business outcomes.

This guide provides a focused structure for preparing portfolio stories, architecture discussions, coding conversations, and leadership examples without relying on memorized answers.

## Positioning statement

A strong senior engineering introduction should be concise, evidence-based, and aligned to the target role:

> I am a senior software engineer focused on building reliable .NET systems, Azure-native platforms, and pragmatic engineering practices. I bring hands-on delivery experience, architecture judgement, production ownership, and a strong interest in using AI-assisted engineering responsibly to improve quality and developer productivity.

Use the introduction to establish four themes:

- **Technical depth:** .NET, C#, APIs, distributed systems, cloud architecture, testing, observability, and secure delivery.
- **Production ownership:** Reliability, incident response, deployment safety, monitoring, rollback, and operational readiness.
- **Technical leadership:** Code review, mentoring, standards, documentation, decision records, and cross-functional communication.
- **Modern delivery:** AI-assisted engineering, automation, developer productivity, and continuous improvement.

## Story bank

Prepare a small set of reusable examples using the STAR format: situation, task, action, result. Each story should include the context, constraint, decision, trade-off, and measurable outcome where possible.

| Story theme | What to demonstrate | Preparation notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Architecture decision | How you evaluate options and choose a path under constraints. | Include alternatives considered, risks accepted, and why the decision fit the business goal. |
| Production incident | How you respond calmly, communicate clearly, and prevent recurrence. | Explain detection, mitigation, root cause, follow-up actions, and operational learning. |
| Legacy modernization | How you improve systems incrementally without risky rewrites. | Discuss test coverage, seams, migration strategy, stakeholder alignment, and release safety. |
| Technical leadership | How you raise team standards through influence rather than authority. | Cover mentoring, review habits, documentation, decision frameworks, or engineering rituals. |
| AI-assisted delivery | How you use AI tools responsibly while preserving engineering accountability. | Mention validation, security boundaries, tests, review discipline, and human ownership. |

## Architecture interview checklist

Architecture interviews should show structured thinking more than a single perfect design. Start by clarifying requirements, then make trade-offs explicit.

1. **Clarify outcomes:** users, business goal, core workflows, success metrics, and constraints.
2. **Define requirements:** functional scope, scale, latency, availability, security, compliance, and data retention.
3. **Shape the domain:** bounded contexts, APIs, ownership boundaries, and integration points.
4. **Select architecture:** monolith, modular monolith, microservices, event-driven design, background processing, or hybrid approach.
5. **Choose Azure services carefully:** App Service, Functions, Container Apps, AKS, Azure SQL, Cosmos DB, Service Bus, Event Grid, Key Vault, Application Insights, and managed identity.
6. **Design for failure:** retries, idempotency, timeouts, circuit breakers, dead-letter queues, graceful degradation, and rollback.
7. **Plan operations:** telemetry, dashboards, alerts, runbooks, SLOs, incident response, and cost visibility.
8. **Explain trade-offs:** complexity, delivery speed, team skill, vendor lock-in, data consistency, and long-term maintainability.

## Coding and craftsmanship preparation

For coding interviews and technical screens, emphasize maintainable implementation over clever shortcuts.

- Write clear, intention-revealing C# with small functions and meaningful names.
- Explain complexity, edge cases, null handling, concurrency concerns, and failure paths.
- Use tests to describe behaviour, not implementation details.
- Discuss when a simple data structure is sufficient and when performance constraints justify complexity.
- Refactor after correctness is established.
- Treat AI-generated suggestions as drafts that require review, tests, and repository alignment.

## Behavioural and leadership prompts

Use behavioural questions to demonstrate judgement, collaboration, and ownership:

- Tell me about a time you disagreed with an architecture decision.
- Tell me about a production issue you helped resolve.
- Tell me about a time you improved engineering quality or delivery flow.
- Tell me about mentoring an engineer or raising team capability.
- Tell me about balancing short-term delivery pressure with long-term maintainability.
- Tell me about introducing a new tool, practice, or AI-assisted workflow responsibly.

For each answer, make the decision process visible: what you knew, what was uncertain, who you involved, what you chose, and how you validated the outcome.

## Questions to ask interviewers

Strong questions show senior-level ownership and help assess fit:

- What are the most important engineering outcomes for this role in the first six months?
- Where are the biggest reliability, delivery, or architecture constraints today?
- How does the team make and document technical decisions?
- What does production ownership look like for engineers?
- How are code quality, testing, observability, and deployment safety measured?
- How is AI-assisted engineering being adopted, governed, or evaluated?
- What support exists for visa sponsorship, relocation, and long-term growth?

## Preparation routine

Use a repeatable preparation loop before each interview:

1. Review the role description and map it to relevant stories.
2. Select three portfolio examples that demonstrate senior impact.
3. Practice one architecture walkthrough using the target company's likely domain.
4. Refresh core .NET, Azure, SQL, API, testing, and distributed systems fundamentals.
5. Prepare questions about engineering culture, production ownership, leadership expectations, and sponsorship process.
6. Capture follow-up notes immediately after the interview for continuous improvement.
